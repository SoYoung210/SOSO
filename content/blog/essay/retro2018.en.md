Now that 2018 is wrapping up, it's time to write my **deploy** post.

> I was going to call it a "retrospective," but I scraped together some developer wit and went with Deploy instead ..

![image-201812311411198](./images/image-20181231-1.png)

Before writing my 2018 retrospective, I went back and reread my 2017 one. Sure enough, 2018 was even more eventful than 2017, and I'm wrapping up the year having somehow taken my first steps as a developer.

Here's a rundown of 2018, just the headlines.

1. A project I worked on somewhere between a planner and a developer failed to ship
2. Got rejected for a department transfer (a major crisis)
3. Finished the required computer science courses in the first semester.
4. Got rejected from developer internships and went back to being a planner (ugh, I want to be a developer!)
5. Hell of a second semester (about my aptitude...)
6. I'm going to get a job. Suddenly, but firmly, started job hunting.
7. My first time as a PM, the YAPP project
8. I need to work harder.
9. In 2019 I want to accomplish OOO and find an answer to OOO.

When the year began, my plan was **"finish third year well, then run hard in fourth year!"** — but in the end, I only finished third year and went on leave from school again.

## Planning with Phaser.js (a Spoonful of Dev)

### 2017.11~2018.02 / 2018.07~2018.08

![image-20181231141119378](./images/image-20181231-2.png)

At work, I took on the role of planning text-coding educational content built with `Phaser`.

The service's goal was to wrap Phaser functions properly so people could produce fun results with simple code, but my lack of JS knowledge meant I couldn't build it myself.

That's when I started seriously dreaming of becoming a developer, driven by the thought, **I want to build the things I imagine, myself!** I kept asking the developer I worked with, "How did you build this?" and wanted, **intensely**, to learn JS.

> Back then, this service's code felt like a real Everest to me. Looking back, I think what I wanted, by the end of this year, was to become someone who could understand this service's code.

It's confidential so I can't go into detail, but this service never actually shipped in the end. It left me with a goal: I want to meet users with something I've imagined — and built — myself.

## Failed Department Transfer.

### 2018.02

The transfer to Computer Science — the whole reason I'd taken leave — failed. The reason was my GPA being lower than other applicants'. I resented my past self a lot, and considered things like transferring schools too, but since I hadn't prepared a Plan B early enough, I had to go straight back to school as-is. I wanted the transfer intensely, out of fear of studying physics and enjoyment of studying computer science, and I failed.

> This is with the benefit of hindsight, but thinking back, failing the transfer made the second semester incredibly hard, and that ended up being the powerful trigger that made me want to join the company I work at now.

## Failed Developer Internships, Back to My Original Company.

### 2018.06~2018.08

With the vague thought of **a developer, this summer!** I applied to internship openings at some big-name large corporations. The tests were algorithm-heavy, and because I hadn't prepared enough, I got poor results across the board and went back to the company I'd worked at earlier in the year.

This became yet another moment that made me intensely want to live as a developer.
My time at the company was spent, not on handling issues for the project I was building alongside developers, but on finishing up documentation as a planner, and I found the whole process incredibly boring. I wanted to be the one reporting bugs as a developer, and to be part of the process of actually fixing them.

_"At this rate, I don't think I'll ever become a developer."_ — driven by that anxiety, I started learning React through a course. Using my lunch breaks, mornings, and weekends to watch the lectures and type along, I learned it to roughly the level of _oh, so this is what React is._

And with that, I said a complete goodbye to the company I'd been working at.

## Hell of a Second Semester, and the YAPP Project

### 2018.09 ~ 2018.11 (1)

First semester was bearable since the classes were mostly programming-focused. I thought, "Oh, I can actually pull off this whole diligently-attending-classes life!" — but now, to graduate, I had to keep grinding through physics-department credits.

And I joined a university student club called YAPP as a front-end developer. [Still ongoing.](https://github.com/YAPP13-4)

Having studied development on and off, I'd picked up bits and pieces here and there, and I wanted to actually put them to use collaborating with other people — and naturally, I ended up taking on a Manager role alongside that.

It was my first time in a Manager role, so I was clumsy from the start, and I'm still trying to find an answer to **what good managing even is.**

> About midway through the project I wrote a [mid-project retrospective](https://github.com/SoYoung210/Study_note/blob/master/Project/React/retrospect.md), and based on that I gave a short talk at GDG Campus Korea Lightning Talk.

![Screenshot 2018-10-12](https://github.com/SoYoung210/Study_note/blob/master/Project/React/%E1%84%89%E1%85%B3%E1%84%8F%E1%85%B3%E1%84%85%E1%85%B5%E1%86%AB%E1%84%89%E1%85%A3%E1%86%BA%202018-10-12%20%E1%84%8B%E1%85%A9%E1%84%92%E1%85%AE%208.27.29.png?raw=true)

It was my first project actually using React, something I'd only ever learned through video courses, and running a real project that put those early lessons to use taught me a lot. It gave me a chance to rethink how I approach learning.

Thanks to meeting great teammates, we even managed a great result — 2nd place — at the final awards ceremony, and we're still developing it further and preparing to ship it!

> The YAPP team is truly the best team. I feel so sorry that my managing wasn't up to par, but they always say, "Soyoung, we're doing this together, right~" and we keep growing together.

The project was so much fun, and life at school was so hard, that I had no confidence I could keep enduring it next year. **Suddenly, but firmly,** I decided I wanted to live as a developer next year, and started preparing for it.

### 2018.09 ~ 2018.11 (2)

Around October, schoolwork got unbearably hard.

```
Why do I even have to study this? I find studying development fun.. no matter how hard I try, I'm just not interested in physics...it's not that I hate studying.....
```

With thoughts like that, every day I went to school felt meaningless, and I poured nearly all my time into YAPP development instead. (Roughly 5 hours a day, 5-6 days a week, I think.)

As I saw it, objectively, the thing I was supposed to be doing was schoolwork. Thoughts like, well, I'm a student, so of course studying comes first.

But by abandoning the objective task and choosing to study development instead, I couldn't produce any measurable indicator at all. I couldn't complete a sentence like `If I study development, ___` the way I could complete `If I study, I get good grades.` (Because I was, after all, a student.)

**I wavered.** As my self-esteem dropped day after day, the question "Can I actually become a developer?" kept nagging at me. I wanted to find an answer to _I'm spending most of the week studying development like this... what can I actually achieve?_

Wanting to turn that final `?` in **"Can I become a developer?"** into a `.`, I started job hunting early.

## Job Hunting

### 2018.11

Since growth was my goal, I focused my job search on startups and started preparing for it.

I reflected on how naive it was to vaguely think job hunting just meant `algorithms`, and after recognizing the importance of **expressing myself well in writing**, I worked hard putting together my cover letter and project write-ups.

I got a lot of help from a lot of people through this process. Without other people's help, I honestly can't say for sure whether I'd be at the company I work at now.

> Probably **not,** most likely.

It was a period where, just by writing up things I'd been using without thinking, I ended up learning a lot of the theory behind them. It became the moment I could finally say I **knew at least a little of what I was using**, for JS, React, and everything else.

And around the time I was job hunting, there were people preparing to switch jobs who asked me to share the notes I'd organized in markdown.

It felt proud and grateful to know my notes could be even a little bit helpful.

And I found an answer to one question I'd always wondered about.

```
Q. I've gotten help from so many people.. how do I pay it back?
```

My own answer to this question is below.

```
A. When someone asks for help the way I once did, I'll help them with my whole heart too.
```

That's the conclusion I landed on, and as a first step I decided to start running a [blog](https://sosolog.netlify.com/).

![image-20181231150449164](./images/image-20181231-3.png)

There aren't many posts on it yet, but I want to share a lot more in 2019.

## Got a Job!

### 2018.12.26 ~ ing

![image-20181231150819521](./images/image-20181231-4.png)

Right at the tail end of 2018, I joined a company as a developer.

I had a lot of fear about `what does it even feel like to work at a company as a developer?`, but now I want to hold onto that same feeling and run hard, with no regrets.

**Now that I've finally started the thing I wanted and hoped for so much, I want to build a journey through 2019 that I can look back on proudly.**

## TMI

## What About 2019?

I'll wrap up this retrospective by thinking about what kind of person I want to be in 2019 — ~~though setting goals doesn't mean they'll all come true~~.

### Growing at the Company

I want to understand the code at the company I work at now, add my own code to it, and ship features. I have a goal of contributing, **as a developer,** to a service used by countless users.

Rather than just saying I'll work hard, I thought it'd be better to set out concrete methods, so I sketched a few out briefly.

- Whenever something raises a question, log it in the agenda, then document it in markdown.
- If there's something worth studying deeply, write it up and publish it to the internal Confluence and the blog (excluding anything confidential).
- Don't pretend to know things — boldly ask about what I don't know, and share it with everyone.
- Bring it on! Clean Architecture, Python, GraphQL

### A New Community

Preparing to become a developer was full of hard stretches, and I ran into a huge amount of information I wish I'd shared with other people earlier. I felt that the chance to come across that kind of information was hard for students to find, especially non-majors.
(It's possible if you search hard enough, but still..)

So I started a [new community, For.D](https://www.facebook.com/ForDeveloperKorea/).

I hope next year is a year where I can grow this community while giving back at least a little of the help I received.

### I Want to Give a Talk.

Going to developer events, I started having this vague thought: someday I want to stand on that stage myself and share something great with people.

Encouraged by the post [Let's Try Speaking at a Conference](https://medium.com/@Jbee_/%EC%BB%A8%ED%8D%BC%EB%9F%B0%EC%8A%A4%EC%97%90%EC%84%9C-%EB%B0%9C%ED%91%9C%EB%A5%BC-%ED%95%B4%EB%B3%B4%EC%9E%90-2ed22eb59f7f), I want to make my debut as a speaker, built on everything I've diligently organized from what I've learned.
